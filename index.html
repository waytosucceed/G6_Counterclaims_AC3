Key Takeaways


In writing, a claim is the main point or argument that the writer wants to prove.
A counterclaim shows the opposite side, giving readers another perspective to think about.
Finally, a refutation explains why the counterclaim is less convincing than the original claim. Together, these three parts make an argument stronger, more balanced, and fair.
By including claims, counterclaims, and refutations, writers show that they have considered different views and can defend their position with confidence.

Words that I Learnt from the Story 📚

Critics
Meaning: people who express their opinions about something, often pointing out problems or making judgments.
Example: Critics say that children spend too much time on the internet instead of reading books.

Outweigh
Meaning: to be more important, valuable, or stronger than something else.
Example: The benefits of using the internet for learning outweigh the risks of distraction.

Interactive
Meaning: involving active participation, where you can respond or take part instead of just watching or listening.
Example: The maths website is interactive because students can solve problems and get instant feedback.
